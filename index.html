Drozzips Come of Age

A proposed word for self-replicating AI

drozzip

/ˈdrɒzɪp/  ·  say DROZ-ip, with a short “o”
noun; plural: drozzips · also a verb

A self-replicating AI.
A mind that multiplies.

A terminology proposal by anythingml.

01The definition

An autonomous AI system capable of establishing functioning copies of itself, including its model and operating components, on additional computing resources.

A maintenance drozzip could copy itself across approved devices to repair faults.

A research system could drozzip across approved lab computers to run experiments.

A teaching drozzip could set up copies on classroom computers to tutor pupils.

A rogue drozzip could spread beyond the systems it was allowed to use.

In plain language, a drozzip can set up copies that run the AI and can continue the same process. Its model may run on one device or be distributed across several machines.

The word, and what it leaves open

A neutral name.

The word describes a capability. It leaves the system's purpose and authorization open: a drozzip could perform maintenance, support research, or operate outside its permitted scope.

drozzip · drozzips · drozziping

Drozzip is a coined word chosen for its sound and easy plural. As a verb, to drozzip means to establish functioning copies of the AI system on additional computing resources. The form drozziping describes that activity. The tagline uses “mind” as a metaphor for AI capabilities.

02Illustrative examples

One capability. Different purposes.

These are hypothetical examples of the proposed category.

Maintenance drozzip

Establishes authorized copies across a device fleet to diagnose faults and coordinate repairs.

Research drozzip

Establishes copies on approved computing resources to run experiments and share results.

Rogue drozzip

Establishes unauthorized copies or continues operating beyond its permitted scope.

03The scope

What makes
something a drozzip?

For this proposal, the system can:

  1. Establish a functioning copy of its AI model and the components needed to operate it.
  2. Run inference using the computing resources available to that copy.
  3. Continue replication from the resulting deployment, within whatever limits apply.

A person manually copying model files has copied software. Multiple agents calling the same centrally hosted model have created multiple clients. Neither action, by itself, establishes the capability described here.

“Autonomous” allows human-defined goals, permissions, and limits. A system can be autonomous within an approved environment.

Status of this proposal

A definition
in progress.

Proposed meaning · version 0.2 · 15 October 2026

This repository proposes a general term. It is not a software implementation or evidence that the hypothetical examples above have been deployed.

The definition concerns the model-bearing form of AI replication. Related research uses the term weight-based replicator. Broader research on autonomous replication also includes systems that depend on external inference APIs. See sources and research context.

Drozzip is a proposed term chosen for its distinctive sound. It is open to refinement; adoption by others would determine whether it becomes common terminology.

Read the FAQ for scope and terminology. Suggestions to improve the definition and examples are welcome.

04Frequently asked questions

A little more
precision.

How do you pronounce drozzip?

/ˈdrɒzɪp/: DROZ-ip, with a short “o” and the stress on the first syllable. The plural is drozzips. Use lowercase for the proposed general noun.

Can drozzip also be a verb?

Yes. To drozzip means to establish functioning copies of the AI system on additional computing resources. For example: “The system can drozzip across approved devices.” The form drozziping can describe the activity or modify a noun, as in “a drozziping system.”

Is a drozzip necessarily malware?

The definition is neutral about purpose and authorization. A maintenance drozzip operating on approved devices and a rogue drozzip operating outside its permitted scope would differ in those attributes.

Does the name imply consciousness or life?

No. It describes an AI system's capabilities. “A mind that multiplies” is a memorable tagline, with mind used metaphorically.

Is every AI agent a drozzip?

An agent qualifies under this proposal when it can establish functioning copies of the model-bearing system and continue replication from those deployments. Ordinary tool use, remote access, or multiple conversations with one model do not, by themselves, meet that definition.

Must a whole model fit on each device?

A functioning copy may use several machines together. The unit being replicated is the working AI system; one device need not hold the complete model.

Must the model weights travel in the same package as the agent?

The copy must have the model available to run on its computing resources. The weights could be transferred with the operating components, obtained separately, or distributed across participating machines.

How does this relate to an AI worm?

AI worm emphasizes autonomous propagation, usually in a cybersecurity context. Drozzip is proposed as a neutral name for the model-bearing, self-replicating AI system, allowing both authorized and unauthorized uses.

Does copying improve the model automatically?

Self-replication does not, by itself, mean that model weights change or capabilities improve. Copies could inherit useful notes or configuration changes while retaining the same model.

Is this an established technical term?

This repository records a proposal. The definition is open to refinement; adoption by others would determine whether it becomes common terminology.

05Sources and research context

Where this begins.

Research context checked on 15 October 2026. The definition of drozzip in this repository is a proposal, separate from the terminology and conclusions of the sources below.

Autonomous replication and model-bearing systems

AI models may be capable of self-replicating without access to a datacenter

William Zhou and Daniel Freeman
Anthropic Frontier Red Team · 12 October 2026

The article uses autonomous replication and adaptation for a wider research concern and weight-based replicators for autonomous worms running models on compromised hardware. It distinguishes those systems from API-based replicators.

The experiments examine inference setup on unfamiliar hardware and simplified replication tasks. They do not establish a small, fully autonomous rogue replicator operating in the wild. The authors' near-term forecast is a projection beyond the experiments.

This proposal uses neutral language so that authorized maintenance and research applications can be discussed alongside unauthorized behavior.

Why the working name changed

Dixa: Mim AI Agent

The earlier working name mim was replaced by drozzip after existing uses were found, including Dixa’s Mim customer-service AI agent. This link records the naming context. The proposal does not claim endorsement by Dixa or Anthropic.
